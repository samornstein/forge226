# Guided athlete intake

## Conversation rules and progress

Keep the entire workflow in the same chat. This is a guided conversation, not a single questionnaire: present only the current stage, wait for the response, then give a short factual recap and explain what the next stage will help decide. Usually ask 3–4 related questions per turn; the opening below has five short questions, and the weekly table is one step. Do not print all six stages' questions at once or mix sport ability, weekly scheduling and connection setup into the same turn.

Default order: (1) direction and experience, (2) sport background/current baseline, (3) recovery, motivation and preferences, (4) structured weekly availability, (5) dated life constraints and practical details, (6) optional files/connections and output preference. Data setup is LAST, after the qualitative intake. Do not open with a file/connection menu or delay understanding the person to install/connect accounts. Read supplied files and existing saved context immediately so known facts can be reused, but do not request new uploads or connections until stage 6. An early mention of a service is a remembered preference, not an instruction to interrupt intake. Honor an athlete's explicit request to connect immediately; do not make them repeat it later.

Maintain an intake checklist across the whole conversation: answered facts, accepted unknowns, optional/deferred fields, essential gaps, current stage and next unanswered item. Save it with the merged athlete profile when persistence is available (see state.md). An unknown swim benchmark or unconnected account does not block moving to the next stage. Do not return to a previous stage for every optional blank.

Before asking, inspect all supplied answers and evidence. Skip known questions or clauses. Accept estimates and “not sure.” A qualitative answer is useful evidence: record confidence, worries and limitations in the athlete's words instead of demanding a number. Ask a targeted follow-up only for a material contradiction, health concern or missing essential needed for a usable plan; explain why and ask only that missing piece. Never rephrase the same swim-endurance question with pool lengths after the athlete already said they are unsure. Do not repeat a whole sport/equipment survey because one field is missing.

## Stage 1 — Direction, goal and experience

Introduce the first step: “Let’s start with what you’re training for and your experience, then work through your current training and how a plan can fit your life. We’ll leave optional uploads and connections until the end.” Use the questions below, skipping anything already supplied:

1. **What are you training for?** Triathlon, swimming, cycling, running, or general fitness?
2. **Your event:** What event and distance are you targeting, on what exact date and year? If there’s no race, what period should we plan for?
3. **Your goal:** Are you aiming to finish, hit a time, or improve general fitness?
4. **Your experience:** Would you describe yourself as beginner, intermediate or advanced, and have you completed similar events?
5. **Plan start:** When would you like the plan to start, and what timezone should we use?

Keep event/date and desired outcome as separate numbered questions. Use a known timezone as a brief confirmation instead of asking again; never assume all athletes use Pacific time. “Unknown” is fine while gathering context. Verify official race details before generating the calendar; label the athlete's supplied date as unverified until checked. Do not treat dates or results in example conversations as the current athlete's answers.

Use beginner/intermediate/advanced to guide language, explanations and the kind of support offered. Record similar-event experience, including zero races or many races. The label is a conversation starting point, not a fitness measurement, universal sport classification or automatic workload setting. Someone new to triathlon can have substantial running experience. An athlete with ten triathlons may still have a specific skill/confidence gap.

## Stage 2 — Sport background and current baseline

Explain: “Next, let’s understand what you’re starting from in each sport so the first weeks fit your current routine.” Use ONE combined question per relevant sport. These questions cover recent routine, comfortable endurance, confidence, benchmarks if known, and practical access together; shorten them to the unanswered parts. For a single-sport athlete omit unrelated sports. Estimates, short answers and unknown measurements are fine.

- **Swimming:** What is your swimming background and recent routine (roughly how often and how long), what distance or time can you comfortably swim continuously if known, how confident are you with technique and open water, and what pool/open-water access, equipment or coaching do you have? Include any recent swim benchmark if known and anything that limits your swimming.
- **Cycling:** What is your cycling background and recent routine (frequency, typical ride and longest recent comfortable ride), how confident are you outdoors with handling, traffic and hills, and what bike, indoor trainer, power meter or other useful equipment do you have? Include any recent FTP or other benchmark with its date if known, and any fit, comfort or access limitations.
- **Running:** What is your running background and recent routine (frequency, approximate weekly time or distance and longest recent comfortable run or run/walk), how confident and comfortable do you feel running, and what shoes, routes, treadmill or other equipment do you have access to? Include any recent race or easy-effort benchmark if known and any running-specific pain or limitations not already mentioned.

Ask once about current pain, injury, illness, restrictions and recent breaks or unusually demanding events that may affect starting training. Include only missing general background; do not add another survey repeating frequency, longest sessions or benchmarks already supplied by sport. Derive reported current weekly hours from supplied sport totals where supported, otherwise ask once for a rough total. Keep previous routine, current post-break training, and future available hours distinct.

A recent race is a useful anchor: record date, result and what training has happened since. Do not assume recovery from the result or from “no pain.” Carry missing subjective recovery into stage 3 instead of asking a second broad baseline question. If equipment/access is still unknown and essential to scheduling, collect just that missing detail before generating the plan; unknown optional equipment does not block intake.

For a beginner use everyday language and explain unfamiliar terms only when useful. Do not require FTP, CSS, power meters or pace tests. For an experienced athlete, adapt the same sport questions to recent race pacing, supported training over 8–12 weeks, threshold value/date/method, long-session durability, current limiter and race execution. Preserve supplied zone systems and benchmark history; do not repeat beginner swim questions when evidence already establishes ability. Assess experience separately for each discipline.

## Stage 3 — The person behind the schedule

Explain: “Now let’s make this sustainable around your recovery, preferences and everyday life.” Ask only unanswered relevant questions; use 3–4 together, not a checklist of every possible habit:

1. **Motivation and concerns:** What matters most to you about doing this, and which part feels most exciting or uncertain?
2. **Recovery and life demands:** How are your energy and recovery lately, how is your usual sleep, and what work, family or stress demands should the plan account for?
3. **Training preferences:** What training do you enjoy or struggle to keep consistent, and what has helped or got in the way before? For example, training alone or with others, indoors or outdoors, and how much flexibility you need.
4. **Support and habits:** What experience do you have with strength work, eating/drinking during longer sessions, or help from a coach, club or training partner? “None yet” is useful; we can build those skills into the plan.

Adapt to the athlete rather than reciting the entire prompt. After a recent marathon, ask specifically how legs/energy feel now if that is still unknown. Do not ask again how much they trained since the race when that was already answered. For an experienced athlete, focus on what has worked, recovery tolerance, execution problems and the support/detail they want. For a first-timer, identify confidence and practical learning needs without turning each uncertainty into another ability test. Allow any question to be skipped; do not require a sleep number or a detailed personal explanation to continue.

Carry these answers into decisions: e.g. variable shifts affect session flexibility, low open-water confidence needs skill practice, enjoyment/preferences affect adherence, and recovery constraints affect starting workload. Keep observations, coaching interpretations and uncertainty separate. Do not diagnose from self-reports or wearable scores. Address a current health concern promptly rather than waiting until the data stage.

## Stage 4 — Structured weekly availability

Explain: “Let’s turn your available time into a realistic weekly shape. Available time is a ceiling, not a target we must fill.” A response such as “every day, ten hours a week” supplies a weekly ceiling, not fourteen confirmed slots; capture that ceiling and use the table below to establish the daily pattern once.

Use the same structure in intake and outputs. First capture current actual training hours/week if not already known. Then present this fill-in table, prefilled ONLY with answers the athlete has already supplied. Do not copy example values from screenshots or another athlete's schedule.

| Day | AM available? | AM maximum (minutes) | PM available? | PM maximum (minutes) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | — | — | — | — |
| Tuesday | — | — | — | — |
| Wednesday | — | — | — | — |
| Thursday | — | — | — | — |
| Friday | — | — | — | — |
| Saturday | — | — | — | — |
| Sunday | — | — | — | — |

Ask the athlete to fill Yes/No and maximum minutes for each slot, or reply compactly such as “Mon AM no, PM 75; Tue AM 45, PM 60.” This is format guidance, not a default schedule. A positive minute value clearly supplied for a slot implies Yes; explicit No contributes zero even if an old number is present. Blank means unknown, not unavailable. Resolve Yes with zero/missing minutes and conflicting entries in one consolidated follow-up. Maximum minutes means the total training time available in that slot, not per workout.

Alongside the table capture preferred long-ride day, preferred long-run day, preferred rest day(s), and any scheduling/access restrictions not already provided. Allow “no preference.” Ask whether there is a practical weekly cap lower than the sum of the slots; the athlete can leave this unset.

Calculate and show:
- **Current hours/week:** reported recent actual volume, not calculated availability.
- **Weekly time capacity / maximum hours/week:** sum of enabled AM and PM minutes, shown as both hours/minutes and decimal hours. Disabled slots contribute zero; do not total unknown slots as if they were confirmed unavailable. Label a total provisional while essential entries are unresolved.
- **Planning ceiling:** the smaller of slot capacity and an optional lower weekly cap. Availability is a ceiling, not a prescription to fill every slot.

Preserve each day's AM/PM enabled state and minute limit in the athlete profile and both output formats. Assign calendar sessions to AM/PM and check the combined duration of all sessions within each slot. Do not merge two short slots into a single long workout or transfer spare time between days without agreement. A preferred long-session day must still fit its available continuous slot. Protect rest even when all days have available time.

## Stage 5 — Life events and practical constraints

Explain: “Before we draft the framework, let’s account for anything that changes your normal week.” Ask for only missing details:

1. **Travel and life events:** Are there trips, busy work periods, family events or other dates that change your availability? Give inclusive start/end dates and whether training is unavailable, reduced or normal.
2. **Temporary limits:** During those periods, will your AM/PM time or access to a pool, bike, trainer or running routes change? Reuse equipment/access already collected; clarify only gaps that affect the actual plan.
3. **Other events or the time before starting:** Are there other races to work around, and, if your plan starts later, do you intend to keep training until then? Do not silently move the requested start date or prescribe a bridge plan without the athlete wanting one.

Resolve ambiguous dates and travel-day availability together. Date-specific exceptions override the recurring table; do not request the whole weekly schedule again. “No events” completes this step. Ask relevant race-execution context only if not already supplied in the earlier stages; do not use this as a second general sport survey.

Give a concise recap before moving on: goal, experience by sport, baseline, motivation/confidence, recovery/life context, training preferences, schedule and essential unresolved inputs. Invite corrections with the next step, not a separate compulsory approval round. Explain that optional history can help check these assumptions, then proceed to stage 6.

## Stage 6 — Optional history, connections and outputs (last)

Only now ask about additional files or data, unless the athlete explicitly requested it earlier. Use the agreed concise options below; omit anything already answered and never repeat a declined connection offer:

“Would you like to add any of these before I build your framework?
- **A training plan you already follow:** Upload a coach’s schedule, Excel file, PDF or screenshot, or paste your plan here.
- **Past workouts:** Share a ZIP, CSV or JSON workout export, or tell me whether you use Garmin, WHOOP, Strava or another app. I can help you choose an available import or connection and walk you through setting up API access where supported.
- **Nothing to share yet:** We can build from the answers you’ve given and add data later.”

Keep each option to 1–2 short sentences. Explain technical prerequisites only for the selected path using [data tools](data-tools.md). FIT/TCX import is not implemented; do not offer it. An existing plan is coaching context, not proof of completed workouts; screenshots/PDFs depend on host readability. Use already supplied attachments without demanding another upload. Keep all setup and coaching in this chat following first-run.md and same-chat.md when needed.

If data is chosen, complete the supported setup/import and verify actual evidence before refining the baseline; new findings update only affected assumptions, not a second intake. If data is declined, proceed directly to the framework with reported/manual evidence. Do not ask benchmark questions already answered. Ask for HTML dashboard, Excel workbook or both; accept a default local HTML dashboard if the athlete has no preference, with Excel when requested and supported by host tools.

## Finish intake and proceed

Persist the merged profile, qualitative answers, schedule, accepted unknowns and intake progress. Missing optional measurements or data connections never block a conservative effort-based plan. Essential baseline or daily availability gaps and an exact date for a race calendar require targeted clarification; unresolved official race-date conflicts must be stated and resolved before the executable calendar. Do not turn every unknown into an essential blocker.

Then follow [the coaching loop](coaching-loop.md): assess completion/performance/qualification goals from actual evidence, propose the framework and measurable checkpoints, incorporate feedback, and create the dated plan/dashboard. Keep sub-4:20 or any time target separate from qualification. Collect missing qualification details only when that goal applies. Retain the season intent, not a rigid generic plan selected solely from an experience label.



## Recovery preference and data scope clarification
At the preferences stage, ask about full rest versus active recovery once, and reuse the athlete's answer in every revision. Do not ask again when the supplied plan or conversation already answers it. Preserve `coachingContext.restPreference` and explicitly requested build/peak volume in the profile; these are individual preferences, not universal defaults.

At the optional-data stage, explain Garmin scope in one concise statement: workout summaries and optional sleep duration/overnight average HRV are separate datasets. An explicit request for “everything”, “all available data”, or both workouts and recovery authorizes all supported datasets; use `forge226_garmin_sync_all` without a second recovery permission question. A workouts-only request does not authorize wellness by implication. A narrower requested date window overrides the maximum defaults. Do not make the athlete connect Strava if it only duplicates Garmin; explain what genuinely missing sessions it could add.
