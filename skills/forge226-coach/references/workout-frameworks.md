# Building swim, bike and run sessions

Use these session families when writing the first plan and when reviewing a block. The examples illustrate main sets. Choose by the athlete's recent experience in that sport, current tolerance, recovery, equipment and goal. A competitive swimmer returning after a break may have advanced technique and limited current endurance. An experienced runner who is new to cycling may need short, easy bike repeats.

## Swim: change speed, then find rhythm
Choose one technical cue the swimmer can carry from a short repeat into longer freestyle. For example, pair 25 of relaxed drill with 25 of whole stroke, then look for the same catch on sustained repeats. Use the pool's actual units and length. Set rest in seconds until a recent repeat pace supports a send-off.

- Learning to sustain freestyle: 6 × 50 relaxed with 20–30 seconds rest. If stroke length shortens, use 25s and take enough rest to recover technique.
- Building repeatability: 6 × 100 steady with 15–20 seconds rest. On alternate reps open briskly for 25, then settle. Compare the first and last steady lengths.
- Preparing for a longer race: 3 × 300 with 20 seconds rest, opening each repeat briskly for 50, then settling to sustainable race effort. Compare splits and stroke count. Extend uninterrupted distance once pacing and technique hold.

A recovery swim can use short technique repeats. A race-specific swim can rehearse changes of pace and settling after a start. Size the whole session to the swimmer's pace and booked lane time, including warm-up, rests and easy finish.

## Bike: choose work minutes, then decide how to distribute them
Write down total ride time, work minutes, target effort and recovery separately. Use current cycling FTP when available. For a rider without a current benchmark, describe breathing, posture and repeatability and collect lap power if available.

- Aerobic skill: 4 × 3 minutes at a comfortable effort, gently increasing cadence for the middle minute. Spin easily for 2 minutes between repeats. Score smooth cadence and stable posture.
- Sustained power: an athlete ready for sweet spot might start with 3 × 8 minutes at the lower end of 88–94% of current FTP. As tolerance develops, compare 3 × 12 with 2 × 18 at the same target. Both give 36 minutes of work. Fewer breaks test longer continuous riding. Include easy spinning between reps.
- Threshold: for a rider already handling hard work, progress from 3 × 8 to 3 × 10 minutes near current FTP with 3 minutes easy between. A later variation can briefly lift above threshold and settle below it to practice pacing rolling terrain. Judge the final repeat against the first before adding time.
- Race rehearsal: select sustainable event effort using previous rides and, for triathletes, the run that follows. Record lap power, time comfortable in aero and subsequent run effort. Keep race-effort minutes distinct from sweet spot minutes.

For example, a 60-minute threshold ride can include 12 minutes warming up, 4 minutes aerobic, 3 × 10 minutes of work, two 3-minute recoveries and 8 minutes cooling down. If an athlete specifies a floor for total ride duration, use aerobic time for the balance.

## Run: choose the stimulus and count the work
Use hills or strides for mechanics, tempo or threshold for sustained speed, track repeats for faster rhythm and selected steady long-run segments for durability. Use current standalone fitness to set training effort. For a triathlete, assess goal pace separately in bricks after representative riding.

- Returning or starting: build easy or run/walk frequency. When easy running is repeatable, try 4 × 15 seconds relaxed and quick on level ground with 75 seconds walking or jogging. Record one minute of quick running.
- Developing: try 3 × 5 minutes comfortably hard with 2 minutes jogging between. Later use measured repeats on a flat course, estimating work minutes from recent splits. Progress only when rep quality and next-day recovery support it.
- Experienced: 3 × 10 minutes and 7 × 1 km can serve similar threshold objectives. The first gives 30 minutes. For the second, multiply seven by the athlete's expected kilometer split, then replace that estimate with the sum of recorded rep times after execution. Use a recovery such as 90 seconds for the tens or 60 seconds for the kilometers when appropriate. A separate track session might use 5 × 800 m at current 5K effort with 2-minute jogs.
- Durability: place one or two steady segments late in an established long run. Progress from 2 × 6 minutes to a longer continuous segment as tolerated. For race-specific bricks, begin easy off the bike and measure a short segment at sustainable race effort. Track these minutes separately from threshold and faster work.

## Place sessions across the plan
Identify one priority session and a shorter alternative for a busy week. Give early blocks a specific skill or aerobic objective even when travel interrupts training. Use a repeatable benchmark across blocks; change one feature at a time when testing progression (work minutes, continuous duration, recovery, posture or pace). Review the combined load across sports, date-specific access and AM/PM capacity. On return from travel, use the last well-tolerated dose as the reference. Scaling can mean changing the session family as well as changing its size.
