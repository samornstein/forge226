# Assess, prescribe, observe, adjust

Use this reference for every new plan and substantive coaching revision. Set `coachingVersion: 1` on new plans. Preserve existing plans; add these fields when next revising them, using current evidence rather than inventing historical judgments. State/schema details are in [state.md](state.md).

## 1. Assessment before calendar

Follow the staged intake in intake.md, including the qualitative recovery, motivation and preferences stage and the AM/PM table. Reuse existing supplied history, skip answered facts, and request optional uploads/connections only at the last stage. Additional imported evidence refines affected assumptions without rerunning intake. No-data athletes can complete the entire workflow.

Choose a primary track: completion, performance, or qualification. Assess experience and current capability separately for each relevant discipline. An experienced cyclist with little swim experience does not receive advanced swim progression. Sustainable recent training, current constraints and the athlete's response govern workload, not the selected label or available hours.

Produce a concise assessment before choosing sessions:
- Baseline: supported recent training, continuity, benchmarks, skill confidence and evidence coverage. Identify what is reported, recorded, calculated or inferred. Training volume alone is not physiological fitness.
- Goal demands: event/course/time demands, execution skills and available preparation time. Missing thresholds do not block an effort-based plan.
- Feasibility: supported, stretch, insufficient-evidence, or reconsider, with a rationale. These are coaching judgments, not probabilities or promises. An insufficient-evidence goal can still receive a conservative plan plus an early checkpoint; an impossible calendar conflict cannot.
- One to three priorities, uncertainties, next action, and the evidence date. Explain how the athlete’s motivation, confidence, preferences, recovery and life demands shape the proposed training; do not merely store those answers without using them. For a first-timer, examples include consistent attendance, comfortable sport-specific endurance, handling and open-water confidence. For a performance athlete, priorities must follow observed limiters rather than default to raising FTP or adding volume.

A race time goal is aspirational until supported by race-specific evidence. When estimating splits, include swim, T1, bike, T2 and run; check arithmetic and show plausible ranges/conditions. Never derive bike speed from FTP alone or triathlon run time from a fresh standalone run alone. Lower and upper split bounds are scenarios, not statistical confidence intervals. Do not prescribe training backward from the desired finish time when it exceeds current capability.

### Qualification branch

Keep a time goal and a championship slot as separate objectives. Record qualifying event, championship year, race-year age group and competition category only when relevant; ask for missing values once. Verify current official event rules, slot allocation, qualification window and ceremony/acceptance requirements. Save URL, retrieval date and rule summary. Recheck for the target season; never hard-code a past allocation system or claim sub-4:20 guarantees a slot.

Research recent official results for the specific course/category when available. Finish results do not reveal which athletes accepted slots: label historical competitiveness separately from a verified qualifying cutoff. Weather, course changes, field and roll-down make projections uncertain. If official evidence is unavailable, save status unverified and identify the missing facts; proceed with training without inventing a qualification probability. Qualification uncertainty does not block a supported fitness plan.

## 2. Evidence-backed training blocks

Use a complete provisional season calendar with detailed next 1–2 weeks. Divide the horizon into dated, non-overlapping blocks with a named focus, rationale and measurable checkpoint. Every training session references its block; every near-term session retains purpose, success cue and a shorter/easier alternative. Long-term entries stay provisional rather than implying precision months ahead.

Checkpoints answer the block's question. They may be a normal session, observed skill, tolerated training pattern or existing race; they need not be maximal tests. Write a criterion that can be assessed from available evidence and set its status to planned until reviewed. Unknown values remain unknown. Do not insert generic numerical targets unsupported by the athlete's baseline.

For completion, prioritize repeatable routines, appropriate endurance progression, confidence, equipment/skills, transitions and practiced race execution. For performance, link chosen sport frequency and stimulus to the actual limiter, include durability/race-position work and rehearsals when appropriate, and account for combined cross-sport fatigue. Do not scale a beginner template upward by hours alone. A strength/fueling/technique priority should have actual scheduled work or execution cues, not only an overview paragraph. Verify authoritative sources for quantitative nutrition or clinical advice.

For each block define conditions to progress, repeat or reduce. The athlete's response and evidence decide; no universal percentage ramp, automatic FTP increase or compulsory three-weeks-up/one-down pattern. Availability remains a ceiling. Preserve effective established training and provide a realistic reduced-time option for key sessions.

## 3. Weekly review and adjustment

Call `forge226_review_summary` for the selected saved plan and requested dates (default trailing seven days), then interpret alongside the athlete's current report. Refresh accounts only when requested. Establish whether supplied data is current before treating an apparent gap as absence.

The review should answer:
1. What was planned and what is confirmed completed, shortened, extended, substituted, missed or unknown? Confirm ambiguous candidate matches together, not one question per activity.
2. Did the key sessions meet their success criteria, and how did the athlete respond? Minutes alone cannot answer this. Ask only for missing relevant effort, pain/illness, fatigue or execution context.
3. Did the block checkpoint change our assessment? Preserve the evidence and revise only the conclusions it supports.
4. What changes next week because of progress, recovery, travel or time constraints?

Save one explicit decision: continue, progress, repeat, reduce, reschedule, pause or reassess. Include evidence, reason, concrete changes (or “none”), date and next review date. Explain the change in weekly hours and key sessions. Modify only affected future dates by default; preserve past prescriptions and actual records. A goal reset, changed race date or sustained disruption may warrant framework revision.

Different triggers need different responses:
- Missing import: leave completion unknown, resolve evidence; do not cut fitness assumptions or add catch-up work.
- One missed session: preserve the main priorities and recovery; do not pile the missed work onto another day.
- Travel: apply dated AM/PM/access overrides and reschedule or substitute only what fits.
- Repeated difficulty: assess execution, load, recovery, benchmark validity and constraints before progressing; one good or bad wearable score does not decide.
- Concerning symptoms: pause demanding work and suggest appropriate assessment; no diagnosis or device-based clearance.
- Successful checkpoint with tolerable recovery: consider a supported progression, not an obligatory increase.

Save decisions even when continuing unchanged, through a new version when recording a formal review. If only answering a question, do not mutate the plan. Respect already-given authorization and existing activation checks. The dashboard is a snapshot: exporting new actuals updates calculated evidence, not the saved coaching assessment or decision.

## 4. Coaching review before activation

Check that priorities follow evidence; the first block starts from current tolerance; near-term prescriptions have usable intensity, arithmetic, recovery, purpose and alternatives; each checkpoint can be evaluated; travel/AM-PM limits and equipment are respected; and the plan addresses execution as well as conditioning. Distinguish unresolved questions from blocking schedule conflicts. The validator checks fields, dates and linkage, not the physiological suitability or truth of the coaching reasoning. Never represent a passing software test as proof the athlete will finish, go sub-4:20 or qualify.
